📦 Last-Mile Delivery Exception Handling

AI-Powered Multi-Agent System — Proof-of-Concept (LangGraph + RAG)

Report Generated: October 4, 2026  |  Data: SQLite (12 customers, 6 lockers) + Delivery Logs + Operations Playbook (RAG)  |  Vector Store: Chroma (36 chunks)  |  Models: gpt-4o-mini (agents) + gpt-4o (critics/judge) + text-embedding-3-small  |  Benchmark: 10 curated shipment scenarios

📋 Business Objective

Goal: Automate the full last-mile delivery exception pipeline — from detecting an actionable exception, to deciding a resolution, escalating to a human supervisor where policy requires, and drafting a personalized customer notification — replacing slow, inconsistent manual triage.

Outcome: ✅ A multi-agent LangGraph system that reasons over delivery logs, customer profiles, locker availability, and a retrieved operations playbook. On the 10-scenario benchmark it reached 100% task completion, escalation accuracy, and tool-call accuracy, with 4.4/5 reasoning coherence.

⚠️ Why Correctness Matters: Each resolution drives real money (reattempt costs, spoilage write-offs, truck rolls) and customer retention. The system makes escalation deterministic, enforces data-privacy at the tool layer (PII only reaches the Communication Agent), and grounds every decision in cited playbook pages for auditability.

🔧 System Architecture

A single orchestrator routes each shipment through specialist agents over a shared, typed state. Deterministic guardrails run before any LLM call; a hard-coded rule engine — not an LLM — owns automatic escalation.

Tools & Data

  • read_delivery_logs — raw status events from CSV (preprocessor only).
  • lookup_customer_profile — SQLite customer lookup with a PII flag (name withheld unless the Communication Agent requests it).
  • check_locker_availability — locker eligibility by zip, size, and capacity status.
  • search_playbook — RAG retrieval over the operations playbook (Chroma), returning page-cited passages.
  • check_escalation_rules — a fully deterministic rule engine for automatic and discretionary escalation triggers.
Reliability by design: noise and prompt-injection are filtered before any model call; a critic/revision loop (max 2) validates resolutions; and a reconciliation guardrail prevents the critic from over-revising a playbook-consistent, trigger-free decision — which eliminated false escalations.

Per-Case Observations

  • SHP-001 & SHP-010 (noise): routine delivery and depot scan are filtered by the preprocessor before any LLM/tool call — correctly non-exceptions, no escalation, sub-second latency.
  • SHP-002 (VIP multi-attempt): duplicate scan deduplicated; reschedule with FORMAL tone; escalated via the rule engine's VIP-history trigger.
  • SHP-003 (address not found): standard customer, no triggers — reschedule with no escalation, demonstrating avoidance of unnecessary escalations.
  • SHP-004 (damaged fragile, VIP): fragile lowers the damage threshold → REPLACE; VIP-history escalation; FORMAL tone.
  • SHP-005 (3rd attempt, locker full): intended REROUTE_TO_LOCKER with mandatory 3rd-attempt escalation; supervisor resolves the full-locker capacity.
  • SHP-006 (refused): RETURN_TO_SENDER, no escalation.
  • SHP-007 (perishable weather delay >4h, VIP): 5-hour delay parsed from free text exceeds the 4h threshold → REPLACE + escalation.
  • SHP-008 (high exception history): full locker on a 2nd attempt falls back to RESCHEDULE; discretionary escalation on the systemic pattern.
  • SHP-009 (severely damaged perishable, premium): leaking/spoiled → do-not-deliver → REPLACE + automatic escalation; FORMAL tone.

📈 Actionable Insights

Deterministic guardrails beat probabilistic judgment where policy is fixed. Moving escalation into a hard-coded rule engine made those decisions exact and reproducible (100% escalation accuracy) rather than depending on LLM mood.
A critic loop needs a brake. Left unchecked, the critic re-revised already-correct decisions and forced false escalations at the retry cap. A reconciliation guardrail — "don't send a playbook-consistent, trigger-free decision back" — fixed the two remaining misses and lifted the system to 100%.
Noise filtering is nearly free and high-value. Routine deliveries and depot scans short-circuit before any LLM or tool call, returning in well under a second and protecting supervisor and compute capacity for the cases that matter.
⚠️ Scope caveat: Results are on 10 curated scenarios with a 12-customer / 6-locker fixture. They demonstrate correctness and consistency of the approach, not production-scale generalization.

📝 Business Recommendations

1. Pilot on live feeds in shadow mode

With 100% on all three hard-accuracy metrics, run the system alongside human dispatchers on one region's real feed — observing, not acting — to confirm accuracy holds on messier, higher-volume data before it drives customer-facing actions.

2. Keep escalation deterministic; humans own the tail

The rule engine, not the LLM, should remain the escalation authority. Every automatic trigger (3rd attempt, VIP history, damaged perishable, fraud address) must continue to route to a supervisor.

3. Instrument with LangSmith as live SLOs

Trace tool-call accuracy, revision-loop frequency, coherence drift, and latency in production. Treat the five metrics as monitored service levels, not one-off evaluation numbers.

4. Expand playbook & benchmark before scaling

Grow the ground-truth set well beyond 10 scenarios (more tiers, package types, multi-region lockers, adjacent-zip reroutes) and version the playbook so RAG always reflects current policy. Re-run the full suite on every prompt or policy change.

5. Quantify ROI against the KPIs

Tie the system to exception resolution time, cost per exception (reattempt, spoilage, truck rolls avoided), and retention. A measured reduction in manual triage time and avoided truck rolls is the number that justifies multi-region rollout.
